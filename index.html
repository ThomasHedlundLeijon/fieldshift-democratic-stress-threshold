Framework

What FieldShift contributes

Most democracy indices describe where a country is. FieldShift is built to estimate when a system is entering a structurally dangerous phase — before collapse becomes obvious.

01 — Warning
Built for early detection, not classification
The central purpose is not to label a country as democratic or authoritarian. It is to identify elevated structural risk before breakdown occurs — typically 3.3 years in advance.
02 — Stress
Institutional stress, not institutional condition
A country can appear democratic on paper while carrying significant structural pressure. FieldShift measures accumulated stress across five domains, not just current democratic stock.
03 — Asymmetry
Quantified erosion–recovery asymmetry
The HL constant ℋ = 1.724 captures the empirical finding that democracies are generally easier to break than to rebuild. Every year of inaction adds ℋ years to expected recovery duration.
04 — Sequence
Sequential institutional firing
Democratic deterioration unfolds in layers. Fiscal integrity weakens first, then constitutional constraints, organizational competition, democratic norms, and finally institutional resilience.
05 — Threshold
Calibrated early-warning threshold
κ ≈ 0.22 defines the boundary of meaningfully elevated risk. It is a warning zone, not a point of no return. Crossing κ creates a window for intervention — before the window closes.
06 — Open
Transparent, testable, replicable
All data, formulas, and intermediate calculations are publicly available. Every claim in FieldShift can be independently verified, critiqued, or challenged. This is not a black-box score.

HL Principle — Core Equations
ℋ = 1 + (Δ/duration × 10)
Recovery = Δ × 1.77 × ℋ × 10 years
Delay cost = delay_years × ℋ
κ = ℋ / 8 = 0.216
Δ is erosion magnitude (peak minus current score). Duration is years of continuous erosion. Every year of inaction adds ℋ years to expected recovery. κ is the empirically calibrated tipping threshold — below this, autocratic acceleration becomes historically likely.

All formulas are open, testable, and reproduced in full in the scientific transparency platform.
