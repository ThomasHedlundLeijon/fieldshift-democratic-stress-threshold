Root Causes of Generic Ballot Bias
MAGA/Shy-Republican Effect (2016–2022)
Trump-aligned voters systematically understate their vote intent in telephone polls. 2022 showed the strongest instance: raw polls showed R+2.8 but actual was D+2.4 — a 5.2 pp miss. Now partially corrected by pollsters adopting education weights.
Likely Voter Screen Timing
Likely voter screens applied months before the election misclassify late-decision voters. Democrats are disproportionately late-registered, younger, and less likely to be flagged as likely voters in early polling — understating Democratic turnout potential.
Non-response Bias (Education Sorting)
College-educated voters respond to polls at higher rates, and since 2016 college-educated voters lean strongly Democratic. Pollsters using education weighting partially correct this, but inconsistency across pollsters creates systematic noise in raw averages.
Herding and Outlier Suppression
When one pollster shows an outlier result, others adjust toward the consensus. This suppresses variance but can anchor the average at a biased point — as seen in both 2016 and 2022 when the consensus was systematically too favorable to Democrats.

📖 Method & Limitations

Generic Ballot Translation Theory (GBTT)

MidShift uses Generic Ballot Translation Theory — a structural model for translating national polling averages into House seat outcomes. Analogous to PollShift's GTT for Sweden, GBTT models the U.S. House as a threshold system where small changes in national sentiment near the majority threshold produce disproportionate seat swings.

GB_tNational generic ballot margin at time t (D−R, positive = D ahead)
GB_effEffective ballot = GB_t + presidential_drag_equiv − bias_correction
HCI_t= clip(0.5 + GB_eff / K, 0, 1) — House Control Index
Seats_D= base_D + presidential_drag_seats + GB_eff × seats_per_pp − gerry_offset
K= 14.8 (calibrated weighted from 5 midterm cycles, 2006–2022)

Limitations
  • National generic ballot is a crude predictor — district-level variance is high
  • Gerrymandering offsets may shift with court challenges or redistricting
  • Presidential drag varies by presidential approval rating (not yet incorporated)
  • No individual district polling incorporated in this model version
  • Demo mode: all polling data is synthetic, not real polling averages
  • Model trained only on 5 midterm cycles — small sample for regression calibration
  • Senate model not included — separate structural dynamics apply
